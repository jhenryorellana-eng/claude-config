# Critical Thinking Reference

Read this **before any creative decision**. This is Step 1 of the workflow — it happens in your reasoning, not in chat, but the Direction Brief in Step 3 must reflect having done it.

The skill's previous version skipped this step entirely and went straight from "what's the topic" to "fill in the boilerplate." That's why every deck ended up identical with different colors. This file is the antidote: a fixed set of questions you must answer for *this specific topic* before you touch a single creative lever.

---

## The five questions

For every deck, before composing the Direction Brief, silently answer these five:

### Q1 — What is the iconic visual?

Not the obvious one. The one that earns a second look.

**Eiffel Tower:** the obvious visual is the silhouette against blue sky. The earned one is the lattice ironwork close-up, the rivets, the perspective looking straight up from the base.

**Machu Picchu:** the obvious visual is the panorama with Huayna Picchu in the background. The earned one is the stone joinery at the Temple of the Sun — where you see the actual craft.

**Notion (SaaS):** the obvious visual is a screenshot of the app. The earned one is a hand-drawn-feel sketch of a database row morphing into a kanban card — the abstraction that explains *why* it's different.

**Coffee:** the obvious visual is a steaming cup on a wooden table. The earned one is the cross-section of a roasted bean, or the bloom phase of a pour-over.

**Healthcare AI:** the obvious visual is a doctor with a tablet. The earned one is the radiologist's screen with the AI overlay highlighting nodes the human missed.

The earned visual is what makes someone pause. If you find yourself reaching for the obvious one, that's a tell that you haven't thought about the topic — you've fallen back on stock imagery in your head.

### Q2 — What is the *one* stat that, if removed, the deck collapses?

Most decks have 3-4 data points. One of them is load-bearing — without it, the argument doesn't stand. The other 2-3 are supporting evidence.

**Identify which is load-bearing.** That stat gets the biggest type, the most generous whitespace, the slide of its own. The supporting stats can share a card or a vertical cascade.

**Machu Picchu:** the load-bearing stat is "1450 — construido durante Pachacútec" (it situates the entire historical claim). The 1.5M visitors/year is interesting but supporting.

**Series A healthcare pitch:** the load-bearing stat is probably "3 hospitales firmados" (validation). The "$230 saved per patient" is supporting. The "47M Americans uninsured" is context, not load-bearing.

**Climate report:** the load-bearing stat is the temperature delta projected by 2050. The annual emissions numbers are supporting.

If you can't identify a single load-bearing stat, the deck doesn't have a thesis yet. Stop and ask the user what they actually want the audience to *take away*.

### Q3 — What is the emotional contract with the audience?

Pick one. Decks that try for two end up with neither.

| Emotion | When it fits | What it does to the design |
|---|---|---|
| **Asombro** | Travel, nature, history, scale, scientific discovery | Generous negative space, slow morphs, hero gets maximum stage |
| **Urgencia** | Climate, market timing, security threats, deadlines | Tight composition, fast cuts, red/warning accents, sparse type |
| **Confianza** | Pitch decks, medical claims, fintech, enterprise | Restrained palette, symmetric grids, real data prominently, no decoration |
| **Curiosidad** | Educational, explainer, interactive | Interactive layouts (radial menu, tabs, zoom portal), reveal-driven motion |
| **FOMO** | Product launch, limited offer, scarcity | Big numbers, countdown vibe, asymmetric tension, single CTA |
| **Indignación** | Advocacy, social commentary, exposé | Brutalist composition, raw documentary feel, monochrome with single warning color |
| **Nostalgia** | Heritage brands, retrospectives, anniversaries | Archival chrome, warm desaturated palette, serif typography, slower motion |
| **Calma** | Wellness, meditation, slow-living, mindfulness | Maximum whitespace, single color family, very slow easing, no sharp transitions |

The emotional contract drives everything downstream — palette intensity, motion preset, copy register, even the slide count.

### Q4 — What is the pivotal slide?

There is one moment in every deck that the rest is built around. It might be slide 3, it might be slide 5. **Identify which slide is pivotal before designing the sequence.**

The pivotal slide gets:
- The biggest layout (full bleed, zoom portal, or oversized hero)
- The longest dwell time in the animation timeline
- The highest-contrast moment in the palette
- The closest to the load-bearing stat from Q2

The non-pivotal slides set up or pay off the pivotal one.

**Machu Picchu deck:** pivotal slide is the Intihuatana zoom portal — the "you are now standing on the sacred stone" moment. Everything else is approach and aftermath.

**Healthcare pitch:** pivotal slide is the moment you reveal "3 hospitales firmados, $4.7M ARR contracted." That's where the room either leans in or doesn't.

**Climate report:** pivotal slide is the temperature curve crossing the 1.5°C line. Everything before is setup, everything after is consequence.

If you can't name the pivotal slide before composing, you don't have a deck yet — you have a list of facts.

### Q5 — What would be the cliché answer, and how do I move past it?

For each of the four previous questions, write down (silently, in reasoning) what the lazy answer would be. Then commit to something else.

| Question | Cliché answer | Move past it by |
|---|---|---|
| Iconic visual | Stock photo of the obvious silhouette | The detail, the cross-section, the moment of use |
| Load-bearing stat | The biggest number | The most *specific* number ("3 hospitales firmados" beats "47M users") |
| Emotional contract | Asombro by default | Pick the one the topic actually deserves — most aren't asombro |
| Pivotal slide | The closing CTA | The moment of evidence — where the argument turns |

The cliché answer is what the average AI deck does. The act of naming the cliché *and rejecting it* is the difference between distinctive and forgettable.

---

## How this connects to the Direction Brief

Your answers to Q1-Q5 directly inform the six lines of the Direction Brief in Step 3:

- **Iconic visual (Q1)** → determines the hero image and which layout to use for it
- **Load-bearing stat (Q2)** → determines which slide gets the biggest layout and the geometry (does it deserve an arc? a sunburst? a single block?)
- **Emotional contract (Q3)** → determines stance, palette intensity, motion preset, copy register
- **Pivotal slide (Q4)** → determines the sequence of layouts (the build-up and aftermath)
- **Moving past cliché (Q5)** → determines chrome philosophy (do NOT default to editorial-rail), grid (do NOT default to centered), and the negative constraints in the brief

If your Direction Brief reads like it could apply to any topic, you skipped this step. Go back and answer the five questions specifically.

## Examples of done-well vs not-done

### Topic: "Mt. Fuji"

**Skipped the critical thinking pass:**
> Direction brief — Mt. Fuji
> • Grid: centered radial → Iconic mountain, deserves to be centered
> • Stance: minimal-poetic → Mt. Fuji is iconic and peaceful
> • Chrome: editorial-rail → Standard travel deck

This is the default. Every line is what the AI would have said for any mountain.

**Did the critical thinking pass:**

(Reasoning: Q1 = the iconic visual is NOT the symmetric snow-capped cone from the lake — it's the view *from the top* looking down at the cloud layer. Q2 = load-bearing stat is "3,776 m" but the more specific one is "1707 — last eruption". Q3 = emotional contract is calma, not asombro. Q4 = pivotal slide is the moment of reaching the summit at dawn. Q5 = cliché is the symmetric postcard view, palette navy+coral, ending with quote.)

> Direction brief — Mt. Fuji
> • Grid:      vertical-thirds → climber's POV — earth, cloud, sky stacked
> • Stance:    minimal-poetic → calma deserves restraint, not asombro
> • Chrome:    invisible → the mountain doesn't need chrome to compete with
> • Geometry:  none (no decorative accents) → silence is the accent
> • Motion:    editorial-slow → ascent feels patient, not energetic
> • Palette:   cold ash + dawn pink (NOT navy+coral) → matches alpine dawn
>
> Forbidden territory (from memory): navy+coral (last 4 decks), editorial-rail chrome (last 3), orbital ring layout (last 2).
> Why this brief avoids it: vertical-thirds grid with invisible chrome puts the mountain alone on stage, which is what calma needs.

The second one came from answering the five questions. The first one came from skipping them.
